BranchRules takes Declared and returns []enforcement.Requirement with Met, Unmet or NotAvailable and never
Waived, because waiving is the project's decision and not something a host reports. Selection is
host.BranchRulesFor, worded like runner.Wrapper's error so the two read alike. The requirement names become
exported constants with Names() in report order, so section 13's budget is held by the compiler for anything
an adapter can spell wrong. Compare walks those names, takes the adapter's answer, treats a declared name the
adapter did not answer for as NotAvailable, and keeps the waive logic and the merge_method line. Protection,
ReviewsExpressible, Fetch, fetchProtection and decodeProtection all go: the last three move with their bodies
intact, the first two have no successor, which is what retiring rather than generalising means. One criterion
could not be built as written and the alternative is recorded rather than the criterion quietly reread.
